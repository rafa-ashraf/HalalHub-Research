1. Executive conclusion

The research points to a consistent architectural pattern across multi-merchant commerce systems: separate the canonical product from merchant-specific offers, use standardized identifiers as the strongest identity signal, govern images as catalog assets, and use automation/AI as supporting layers rather than the source of truth.

What the research supports

GTIN/UPC is the highest-confidence identifier where reliable. Missing identifiers require normalized metadata, fuzzy/semantic matching and eventually image similarity. Merchant images are useful inputs but should not automatically become canonical assets.

What remains an engineering decision

Exact scoring thresholds, provider contracts, storage/CDN choice, AI vendors, review thresholds and implementation sequencing should be validated against HalalHub's actual data volume, merchant feeds and budget.

Important research discipline: the source document explicitly distinguishes publicly documented platform functionality from internal algorithms that are proprietary or not publicly confirmed. The artifact keeps that distinction rather than presenting inferred internals as fact.

3. Proposed catalog architecture

The most important design decision is to stop treating a merchant's listing as the canonical product. A shared catalog product holds stable product facts; merchant records hold store-specific facts.

4. Product identification & matching

The research recommends an identifier-first hierarchy. AI should help resolve ambiguity rather than override strong deterministic identity.

1 · GTIN / UPC exact matchHighest-confidence path when verified.
2 · Metadata matchingMPN, brand, normalized name, product type, size, weight/volume, flavor/variant, package quantity.
3 · Fuzzy / semantic matchingToken similarity, Levenshtein/Jaro-Winkler, n-grams, cosine/TF-IDF and text embeddings.
4 · Image similarityImage embeddings → similarity search → candidate products.
5 · Confidence + reviewHigh confidence → auto-match; medium → review; low → new/candidate product.

Why normalization matters

Merchant A: Astro Original Balkan Yogurt · 750 g

Merchant B: Astro Original Balkan Style Yogurt · 0.75 kg

Normalize to a shared representation before comparing.

Near-match trap: Coca-Cola 355 mL vs Coca-Cola 1.5 L

Similar names do not imply identical products; package quantity and volume matter.

Proposed confidence model

The source document illustrates an explainable score using contributions such as GTIN, brand, name similarity, size, category and image similarity. It also gives preliminary relative weighting in the matching discussion: GTIN/UPC and MPN very high; brand/product type/package size/weight-volume/flavor high; name medium-high; category medium; merchant SKU low for cross-merchant matching.

Key safety valve: fuzzy matching should generate candidates; it should not automatically merge products without confidence and, for ambiguous cases, human review.

Image roles

Primary · secondary · back · side · lifestyle. Each image should carry role and approval status.

Quality standard

Clean background, high resolution, correct product/variant, useful framing, multiple views where appropriate, optimized delivery, no misleading overlays.

AI generation boundary: background removal and enhancement are lower-risk because the real product remains the source. Fully generating a packaged grocery product can introduce incorrect text, ingredients, branding, colors or package size, so it should not be the initial canonical-image strategy.

6. Data sources, AI, infrastructure & cost

Open Food Facts

Useful food-focused source with names, brands, categories, ingredients, nutrition, barcodes and images; quality can vary because data is community contributed.

GS1

Strong foundation for deterministic product identity through GTIN; a GTIN itself does not automatically provide an image or redistribution rights.

Google Shopping

Useful reference for structured identifiers, merchant/product separation and explicit main/additional image concepts.

Commercial providers

Syndigo, 1WorldSync, NielsenIQ and similar providers may offer scale and quality; licensing, coverage, freshness and redistribution rights must be evaluated.

Manufacturer catalogs

Potentially authoritative for GTIN, brand, package size, images, ingredients and nutrition; ingestion may require API, CSV, XML, SFTP, EDI or portals.

AI layer

Background removal, enhancement, OCR, embeddings, image similarity, duplicate detection and quality scoring; use selectively.

Scale example from the research: 1,000,000 products × 3 approved images = 3,000,000 images. At an illustrative 1 MB master image average, that is about 3 TB before derivatives, backups, replicas and metadata. This supports keeping image binaries outside PostgreSQL.

Cost strategy

Start with basic validation and deterministic matching; add image processing and external data selectively; introduce AI only where it provides measurable value. The research emphasizes avoiding expensive AI processing on every image unnecessarily.
